# League rules and calendars

Reference for the WNBA and the leagues WNBA players play in during the offseason. It explains why the analysis measures outcomes **only in WNBA games**, and which offseason details are tracked as context.

_Last researched: October 1, 2026. Sources are listed at the bottom. Items marked **(unconfirmed)** still need a primary source._

---

## Why this matters for the analysis

1. **Outcomes are measured in WNBA games only, per 40 minutes.** WNBA games are 40 minutes (4 × 10), so per-40 is the natural rate. Offseason leagues use different formats (3-on-3, 18-second shot clocks, 2- and 3-point free throws, shorter games), so their stats are **never** compared directly to WNBA stats.
2. **"League" is a label; "load" is the mechanism.** A four-week AU season and a six-month European season are very different workloads. Track offseason **games, minutes, and end date** (rest before WNBA training camp) as context, not just which league.
3. **Season-level shifts affect everyone.** WNBA expansion (13 teams in 2025, 15 in 2026) changes roster depth and minutes league-wide. Compare each player's change to the same season's league-wide change, not to a fixed baseline.
4. **The 2026 season had a mid-season break.** The FIBA Women's World Cup break (Aug 31 – Sep 16, 2026) splits the late season. Define "first half / second half" by **games played**, not calendar dates, and flag World Cup participants (51 WNBA players) as context for 2026.

## WNBA

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | 5-on-5, four 10-minute quarters (40 minutes) |
| Shot clock | 24 seconds |
| Foul-out | 6 personal fouls |
| 3-point line | 6.75 m (22 ft 1¾ in) at the top of the arc, the FIBA distance, adopted in 2013; corners lengthened to match the NBA by 2017 |
| Other differences from FIBA | Defensive 3 seconds is enforced; jump balls on all held balls; no touching the ball on or above the rim (goaltending) |

| Season | Teams | Games/team | Regular season | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 13 (Golden State Valkyries added) | 44 | May 16 – Sep 11, 2025 | First best-of-seven Finals |
| 2026 | 15 (Portland Fire, Toronto Tempo added) | 44 | May 8 – Sep 24, 2026 | World Cup break Aug 31 – Sep 16; playoffs begin Sep 27; new CBA ratified March 2026, expected to grow the schedule toward 52 games over its term |

## Unrivaled (U.S., winter)

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | **3-on-3, full court**, on a smaller court (72 ft × 49.2 ft) |
| Game length | Three timed 7-minute periods, then an untimed 4th period played to a target score (**Elam Ending**: the leading team's score after the 3rd period + 11). The first team to reach the target wins, so there's no overtime. |
| Shot clock | 18 seconds |
| Free throws | One free throw per foul, worth **2 points** (fouled on a 2) or **3 points** (fouled on a 3) |
| Foul-out | 6 fouls, but no one fouls out if a team is down to 3 available players |
| Also | 1-on-1 tournament ($200K grand prize) |

| Season | Teams | Players | Dates / location |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 (inaugural) | 6 | 36 | From Jan 17, 2025, Medley, FL (Miami area) |
| 2026 | 8 (added Breeze BC, Hive BC) | 54 | From Jan 5, 2026 |
| 2027 | — | — | From Jan 8, 2027, touring (Boston, Detroit, Brooklyn, Houston, Philadelphia, and others) |

**Analysis note:** with 3 players on court and an 18-second clock, Unrivaled stats are much higher-usage and higher-pace than WNBA stats. Use Unrivaled **games/minutes** as load context only.

## Athletes Unlimited Pro Basketball (U.S., winter)

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | 5-on-5, four 10-minute quarters, 24-second shot clock, foul-out at 6 |
| Rules base | Close to WNBA/NBA rules, with differences: technicals don't count toward disqualification; jump balls on tie-ups; captain's challenges; carry-over timeouts |
| Scoring twist | Individual leaderboard. Players earn points for team wins (+60 per quarter won, +180 per game won) and individual stats (+20 made 2, +30 made 3, +10 assist/steal/block; −10 turnover or missed shot; −8 to −16 fouls), plus MVP votes |
| Teams | Redrafted every week by the top players on the leaderboard (captains) |

| Season | Players | Dates / location |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 (5th season) | 40 | 4 weeks from Feb 4, 2026, Nashville, TN |

**Analysis note:** the points system rewards individual stats, which may change shot selection. Again, only load is used.

## Project B (global, launching 2027)

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | 5-on-5; seven two-week tournaments across Europe, Asia, and Latin America; 11-player rosters; women's and men's divisions |
| First season | January – April 2027 (Valencia Mar 12–21, Tokyo Mar 26 – Apr 4) |
| Notable signees | Nneka Ogwumike, Alyssa Thomas, Jewell Loyd, Jonquel Jones, Kamilla Cardoso, Sophie Cunningham, Kelsey Mitchell, Azzi Fudd, and others |

**Analysis note:** no data until the 2026–27 offseason. It becomes a new group for the 2027 WNBA season. Its season runs into April, close to WNBA training camp, so rest gap matters.

## Overseas: Europe (FIBA rules)

| | |
|---|---|
| Rules | FIBA: 4 × 10 minutes, 24-second shot clock, **foul-out at 5**, no defensive 3 seconds, ball may be played once it touches the rim, alternating possession instead of jump balls, slightly smaller court |
| Competitions | EuroLeague Women (16 clubs in 2025–26; regular season from October; Final Six Apr 15–19, 2026, Zaragoza), EuroCup Women, plus domestic leagues (Turkey, Spain, France, Italy, Czechia, Hungary, and others) |

**Analysis note:** the longest offseason workload. Players often play both a domestic league and EuroLeague, so the game count can be high. Players also often join late or leave early, so check each player's actual games.

## Overseas: Asia

| League | What we know |
|---|---|
| China (WCBA) | FIBA-based **(unconfirmed)**. 2024–25: 21 teams, up to 3 foreign players per team, season opened mid-November. WNBA players are frequent imports (e.g., Jonquel Jones, Kamilla Cardoso, Jewell Loyd). |
| South Korea (WKBL) | 6 teams. Foreign players suspended since 2020; an Asian quota (Japanese players) was added in 2024–25 and expanded for 2026–27. WNBA players there are mostly Korean nationals. |
| Japan (W League) | 15 teams in two divisions. Foreign-player rules **(unconfirmed)**. WNBA players there are likely Japanese nationals. |
